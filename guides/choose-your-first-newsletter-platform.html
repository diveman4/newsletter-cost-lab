How to choose your first newsletter platform: a 6-question checklist

Most "best newsletter platform" lists rank tools. This checklist helps you decide which one fits your plans before you put your first subscriber on it. It uses only the prices and limits we verified on 2026-11-14.

Links marked * are affiliate links: if you buy through them we may earn a commission, at no extra cost to you. It never changes the numbers. Details.

1. How many subscribers do you expect in 12 months?

Be honest: most first newsletters stay under 1,000 for their first year. If that's you, a free plan covers it on Kit (up to 10,000), beehiiv (2,500) or systeme.io (2,000). MailerLite's free plan stops at 250 and Buttondown's at 100.

2. Will you charge readers?

If yes, the free plans that allow it are Substack (10% cut), Kit (3.5% + $0.30 per transaction) and systeme.io (0% platform fee). beehiiv and Ghost need a paid plan for paid subscriptions, but take 0%. See the break-even table.

3. Do you need automations (welcome sequences, tagging)?

Kit's free plan doesn't include visual automations; its Creator plan does. beehiiv lists automations from its Lite plan. Decide whether a welcome sequence matters on day one or can wait.

4. Is the newsletter the product, or does it support another business?

If the newsletter is the product, publishing-first tools (Substack, beehiiv, Ghost) give you a website and archive. If it supports courses, coaching or products, an all-in-one tool like Kit or systeme.io may save you a second subscription.

5. What will it cost when you succeed?

Put your 12-month subscriber goal into the cost calculator, not today's number. A platform that's free now can cost $100+ a month at 10,000 subscribers.

6. How hard would it be to leave?

Exporting a free subscriber list is usually simple. Moving paid subscribers can be harder because payment setups differ between platforms. If you plan to charge, pick the platform you'd be happy to stay on.
